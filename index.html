A quiet little
world in the sky

Skyseed Isles is a gentle 3D platformer about a sky gardener named Miru who lost her glowing seeds across ten floating islands. Your kid runs, hops and glides to bring them home. Nothing chases them. Nothing punishes them. Falling just means the wind carries them back.

Built for kids who hate waiting

Most games make a five-year-old sit through launchers, updates and login walls. This one opens in a browser tab and starts in about two seconds. It was made for one specific kid whose mom gets home from work at midnight — and for every other kid in the same boat.

01

Nothing can hurt you

There are no enemies, no timers and no game over screen. Fall off an island and a gust of wind sets you back on the grass. Frustration was designed out on purpose.

02

A heroine worth rooting for

Miru is an original character — twin-tailed hair, a white dress with a sky-blue trim and a flower crown. She bobs when she idles and her hair swings when she runs.

03

Treasure, not homework

Glowing seeds sit on every island. Wishing stars hide on pillar tops. Sky rings hang in the air between islands and reward a brave double jump. The counter in the corner tracks it all.

04

Phone, tablet or laptop

On a keyboard it's WASD and space. On a touchscreen a joystick and a big jump button appear by themselves. Same world either way.

05

Quiet by design

Soft chimes on pickups, drifting clouds, waterfalls pouring off island edges into the sky. It's closer to a picture book than an arcade.

06

Open source, open doors

The whole game is readable, un-minified code in one place. When your kid asks how it works, you can actually show them.

From zero to playing in three moves

No downloads, no store, no parental credit card panic.

1

Open the link

Any modern browser works — Chrome, Edge, Safari, Firefox. On a phone, add it to the home screen and it behaves like an app.

2

Press Start Adventure

The camera drifts around the islands while the title screen is up, so even the menu is something to look at.

3

Wander

Run with WASD or the joystick, jump with space or the big button. Press jump again mid-air for a double jump, and hold it while falling to glide.

Things parents ask

Short answers, no fine print.

Does it collect any data about my child?

Almost nothing, and you stay in control. You can play entirely as a guest — no account, no sign-up, progress saved only in your own browser. If you choose to make an account so progress follows your child between devices, we store just a username, an email, a securely hashed password (nobody can read it back) and the game progress itself. No analytics, no advertising, no chat, no selling of data, ever. A parent can delete any account and all of its data from the family dashboard at any time.

Will it run on our old laptop?

Almost certainly. The world uses simple low-poly shapes, so anything that can run a browser from the last five years should hold a smooth frame rate.

Is there anything to buy?

Nothing. No purchases, no currencies, no unlock walls. Every island is open from the first second.

Can my kid lose their progress?

The collected-seed counter resets when the tab closes, which most young kids treat as a feature — the treasure hunt starts fresh every time.

Who made this?

It was built as an open experiment in how far a small, honest browser game can go. The full source sits in a public repository, free to read, copy and remix.
